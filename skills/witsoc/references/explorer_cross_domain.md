# Cross-Domain Explorer Control

Transfer structure, not confidence. Before dispatch, name source and destination
objects, the preserved invariant, translation map, inverse or loss, destination
interpretation, and one falsifier. Use the typed bridge path and retain every
transform receipt.

An analogy is a proposal generator. It becomes a destination-domain candidate
only after local assumptions and meanings are re-established. Source status
does not cross a lossy bridge. Mathematical identifiability does not create
biological identifiability; biological observation does not establish a
mathematical universal. If two bridge branches rejoin, use a declared adapter
and expose nonidentical premises.

Prefer transfers that answer a specific frontier question: reveal an invariant,
construct a discriminator, supply a countermodel, or reduce the open core.
Reject transfers whose destination effect is only thematic similarity. On
return, classify translation validity, destination evidence, and target impact
separately; a failed transfer closes only that translation under its recorded
assumptions.
