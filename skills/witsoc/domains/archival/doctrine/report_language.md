# Report language

Three different statements get written the same way:

```
documented    a surviving source says this
attested      independent origins say this
occurred      this happened
```

The distance between the first and the third is the entire discipline. Prose
crosses it for free, in one sentence, with no new evidence: a single chronicle
becomes "sources record", becomes "it is established that".

## The preferred sentence

> Recorded in [source], at [shelfmark]; [n] independent origin(s) after the
> chains are followed. Bounded by what survived and what was searched.

Boring, and the only sentence the evidence supports.

## The ladder

| From | To | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| documented | attested | more than one origin after the collapse |
| attested | occurred | evidence that the writing tracks the world, not just that it exists |
| record | motive | the intent stated in a source — and then what is established is that someone wrote it |
| instance | generality | an argument about what survived, not a count of instances |

Interior states deserve their own line. "Intended to", "sought to", "believed
that" are not in the record unless someone wrote them down, and when they are,
what the record establishes is that they were written down. That is often still
worth saying — it just is not the same sentence.

## Say what did not survive

Every report here is bounded by what survived, what was searched, and what could
be read. A report that does not say so will be read as unbounded, because readers
default to generality and silence is not a hedge.

## Never say VERIFIED

A surviving document establishes that something was written. That it happened is
a further claim, and no quantity of documents closes that gap. The strongest
thing this pack can say is `CHECKED_BOUNDED_ATTESTED`, and it should be said
about the attestation rather than about the past.
